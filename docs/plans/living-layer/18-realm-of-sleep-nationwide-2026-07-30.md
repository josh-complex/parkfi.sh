## 1. The stakes, named before the fun part

A nationwide layer collides with three foundations. Each collision must be
resolved in the design, not waved at:

1. **Pillar 3 — presence is the currency.** If anything done from a couch
   mints what park presence mints, the currency dies, and with it the
   anti-spoof edifice ([06](06-location-and-geofencing.md)) and priority ①'s
   entire purpose. The one-game directive forces this collision to be
   resolved with a _line_, not a wall — §5's line: **the World's state is
   presence-only; the wielder's growth is presence-weighted.** Communal
   agency (seals, light, marks) stays park-locked forever; personal
   progression flows from both realms into one save, tuned so the park
   day is always the biggest day of a wielder's year.
2. **Pillar 1 — the moat is the live feed.** A nationwide game with
   timer-driven spawns is a worse Pokémon GO with better IP — precisely the
   game doc [01](01-vision-and-strategy.md) says we refuse to build. Whatever
   runs nationwide must still be _driven by the real parks breaking and
   healing_.
3. **Gate zero — license scope.** The KH license was negotiated around an
   in-park experience. A nationwide consumer KH game is a different product
   category to both Square Enix and Disney (and to app-store ratings and
   COPPA exposure — 16 §7.2 grows teeth at national scale). **This is a
   business conversation that precedes any build.** Everything below is
   design-ready contingency for the day that conversation lands.

The thesis that survives those collisions: **the away game happens in a
different realm with its own economy, rendered _from_ the real parks' live
state, and the bridge between realms is held by the players who are
physically there.** KH cosmology doesn't just permit this — it has an entire
canonical realm whose rules match ours one-for-one.

## 2. The cosmology dig — which realm?

Candidates, evaluated against: tone, canonical rules, how cleanly the
realm supports §5's communal/personal line, and how naturally "you, at
home, in Ohio" fits the fiction.

| Candidate                                    | Verdict | Why                                                                                                                                |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Realm of Darkness**                        | ❌      | Villain space; tonally wrong for a cozy at-home surface; "go sit in the darkness" is not a retention pitch                         |
| **The World that Never Was**                 | ❌      | Organization territory; reserved for Rift/endgame fiction (GDD §4.6)                                                               |
| **Station of Awakening / the Dive**          | ◐       | Already claimed as FTUE (16 §1); it's a _moment_, not a place you live                                                             |
| **Traverse Town**                            | ◐       | The canonical refuge between worlds — right tone, but a single town; better as the **hub inside** the winner than the realm itself |
| **The Realm of Sleep** (Dream Drop Distance) | ✅      | See below — the rules align so well it reads like it was designed for us                                                           |

### Why the Realm of Sleep wins, rule by rule

- **Sleeping Worlds are real worlds, mirrored in dream** — cut off from the
  waking realm. Our away game _is_ a mirror of the real parks. The fiction
  and the architecture are the same sentence.
- **What happens in sleep does not touch the waking world.** §5's communal
  line — a dream-seal quiets the dream, never the real wound — isn't a
  compromise we apologize for; it's the realm's canonical physics. Players
  already understand it from DDD. (The heart crossing realms intact is
  _also_ DDD physics — Sora and Riku wake up changed — which is exactly
  the one-save model.)
- **The realm has its own enemy class: Nightmares** (Dream Eaters). The away
  game gets a full bestiary that never inflates the Heartless economy, the
  Journal's waking pages, or spawn balance.
- **And its own companion class: Spirits** — Dream Eaters you _create_ from
  dream fragments and raise. Canonically forged, not caught (pillar 6
  survives review: this is the forge track wearing a dream skin — but flag
  it for explicit pillar review in the canon delta, §10).
- **Traverse Town is canonically the first Sleeping World** — so the hub
  (the Vestibule of 16 §5, upgraded from menu to _place_) sits inside the
  realm with full lore backing.
- **Dropping** (DDD's forced sleep/wake switch) even gives us fiction for
  bounded session design if we ever want it.

**Naming note:** the player-facing frame is **"the Dream"** / diving; the
realm term appears in lore copy. The Dive (16 §1) becomes literally what it
is named: the door into this realm. One fiction, no retcon — the Dive
already established "a dream claims no presence."

## 3. The world model — what "the entire US" actually renders as

The instinct "make the game the entire US" has three possible renderings.
Two are traps.

- **(a) The street-map clone.** Real neighborhoods, POI spawns, walk your
  block. Rejected: it needs nationwide geodata + POI curation (Niantic's
  decade-long, crowd-sourced asset), it moves gameplay onto sidewalks and
  parking lots (a safety/liability surface [09](09-moderation-trust-safety.md)
  was scoped to _parks_ to avoid), it competes with Niantic on Niantic's
  board, and **none of our moat survives the trip** — a spawn on your street
  corner can't be caused by Space Mountain going down without feeling
  arbitrary.
- **(b) The menu game.** Idle/gacha screens with a KH skin. Rejected
  without ceremony: it's not a game, it's a shop.
- **(c) The Dreamscape (proposed).** The Sleeping Worlds are **dream-mirrors
  of the real parks** — the same Worlds, the same geography the save file
  already knows, rendered in the DDD dream palette on the living-map stage
  (workstream ②b: same renderer, dream shader). You dive from anywhere;
  _where your body is doesn't matter, because a dream claims no presence —
  and no address._ No street maps, no national geodata, no sidewalk
  liability. Two light touches keep "anywhere in the US" tangible without
  a map: the dream sky tints with your region's real weather (our weather
  ingest, generalized — pillar 1 in miniature), and the pedometer the
  Capacitor shell already ships lets **waking steps deepen the dream**
  (steps are a dream-side resource trickle: the road you actually walk
  charges dream links — bounded daily, cosmetic-adjacent, never waking
  power).

The deep win of (c): **the live feed still drives everything.** When a real
ride goes down in the waking park, its Sleeping World mirror grows a
**dream-echo** of that breach. Nationwide players are fighting the _shadow
of a real event happening right now_ — the moat, dreamt. No other studio
can build our away game either.

## 4. The away loop (what a dreamer actually does)

- **Dive** (from anywhere) into a Sleeping World — default: the park your
  save file knows best; Dreamers with no save file get Traverse Town + the
  Dive's dream-Shadows until their first real trip.
- **Fight Nightmares** with the same three verbs, same battle theater, same
  `RoundEvent[]` machinery (3D by default, 2D under battery saver — the
  whole ②/②b stack is reused wholesale). Dream-echoes of live breaches are
  the marquee spawns; ambient Nightmares fill the floor between real
  events. **These fights feed the one save**: real XP at the dream rate
  (§5), real Journal pages in the Nightmare chapters, real fragments into
  the one forge.
- **Forge and raise Spirits** from dream fragments (the drop currency of
  Nightmare fights). Spirits are the party members the dream recruits —
  one roster with the Disney companions, realm-tagged: Spirits fight
  anywhere (they're made of dream-stuff and cross freely), Disney
  companions are recruited and fight in the waking parks (§5's exclusivity
  column). One party system, one bond/XP machinery.
- **Tend the dream of the park you love** — sealing a dream-echo resolves
  it _in the dream only_ (the real wound neither knows nor cares), but it
  writes a **dream-vigil** aggregate: "eleven hundred hearts fought for
  Fantasyland in the dream tonight." Where that aggregate is _seen_ is §6.
- **Memory Dives** (17 §4.2) live here naturally — replaying your own
  waking battles is just another dream door. Doc 17's proposal is absorbed
  unchanged as this realm's memory wing.

**The engagement claim, honestly:** this is the same game, played from the
realm you're in — collection (Spirits into the one Journal), mastery (same
combat, harder Nightmare telegraphs), live spectacle (dream-echoes of real
events), identity (your dream of _your_ park), and **real progression on
the one save** at the dream rate. §5b makes completeness for the
never-visitor a hard requirement (a true ending reachable from the couch),
while the DDD palette stays deliberately narrower than the waking parks so
§8's cost analysis survives. One game; the park is where it plays at full
volume.

## 5. One heart, two realms — the presence-weighted economy

> _(Rewritten under the same-game directive — this section previously
> specified a two-economy firewall with one-way physical conversion. That
> model is dead: it made two games.)_

The line, stated as lore: **the World only remembers what happens within
it; the heart remembers everything.** Mechanically: **communal state is
presence-only; personal growth flows from both realms into one save,
presence-weighted.**

**One of each, permanently:** one wielder, one rank, one XP pool, one
Journal (Heartless and Nightmare chapters side by side), one forge, one
roster (Disney companions and Spirits, realm-tagged), one story. There is
no "dream rank," no separate currency wallet, no conversion step — a
fragment farmed at midnight in Ohio and a shard dropped outside Space
Mountain land in the same inventory.

|                         | Waking (park)                                                                   | Dream (anywhere)                                                               |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Communal agency**     | seals, World light, echoes/weaves, emblem registration — **exclusive, forever** | none — dream-seals quiet the dream only; the vigil is an aggregate, not agency |
| **XP → the one rank**   | full rate + condition trophies                                                  | tuned fraction (a balance knob, GDD §8; order of ⅓–½, capped daily)            |
| **Journal**             | Heartless chapters + condition pages (only mintable live)                       | Nightmare chapters, depth pages — same book, fully real                        |
| **Party**               | Disney companions (presence-recruited, park-fighting) + Spirits                 | Spirits (forged anywhere, fight anywhere)                                      |
| **Forge**               | waking materials; signature keychains (first-seal loot)                         | dream fragments; dream-line keychains & gear of comparable-but-distinct power  |
| **Trials (rank bands)** | waking trial forms — the fast, storied route                                    | dream trial forms — every band reachable (§5b), longer road                    |
| **The bridge**          | gate-opening, Wayfinder-sending (§6)                                            | receiving                                                                      |

**The asymmetries that keep the park properly superior — inside one game:**

1. **Only presence touches the World.** The communal layer — the moat's
   social expression — is the park's monopoly. A dreamer grows; only a
   wielder _standing there_ changes what everyone else sees. This is the
   17 §5 refusal, kept whole.
2. **The park is the acceleration and the exclusives.** Full XP rate,
   condition trophies (rain pages, live-status witnesses — unmintable in
   dreams by construction, they're stamped from the resolve-time snapshot),
   Disney companion recruitment, signature keychains, and the waking trial
   forms' regalia. A park day should be worth weeks of dream evenings —
   _worth_, not _required_.
3. **Presence holds the keys to the bridge.** Wayfinders and gate-openings
   are minted only by being there (§6) — the park audience grants, the
   nationwide audience receives.
4. **The dream still points parkward** — dream-echoes captioned with the
   real event, quieted Worlds showing what the waking seal also yields —
   but under the one-game model this is longing inside one life, not
   marketing between two products.

**The integrity consequence (new, load-bearing):** the moment dream fights
mint real XP, the dream inherits the full priority-① regime — sessions,
loadout pinning, server replay, and **rate caps** (the dream's threat model
is botting, not GPS spoofing; it's desk-playable by design, so the replay
validator plus daily caps _are_ the anti-cheat). D1 therefore hard-depends
on priority ① — see §9.

## 5b. The completeness principle — the never-visitor's whole game

> **The requirement:** a wielder who never stands in a park must be able to
> play to a **true ending** — mastery, a full collection, narrative closure
> — entirely within the Realm of Sleep. The park is a second game they may
> someday add. It is never the missing half of the one they have.

### 5b.1 The rule, and its inverse

§5's line said _only presence touches the World_. Completeness adds the
inverse: **no dream door is locked by a waking key.** No dream trial,
Spirit, Journal chapter, depth, or ending may require park presence — and
under the one-game model that promise extends to the spine itself:
**every rank band is reachable through dream trial forms** (the longer
road; the waking forms are the fast, storied route whose regalia —
titles, keychain cosmetics, the deed's realm-stamp — stay park-exclusive).
Fulfillment and parity remain different promises: one game, whole from
either realm; the waking realm is strictly richer — more agency (the
communal layer), faster growth, exclusive companions and trophies — but
never _more finished_.

### 5b.2 The dream's own spine — Sleeping Keyholes and depths

The progression the never-visitor climbs (DDD's structure, almost
verbatim):

- Every Sleeping World descends in **depths** — successive tiers of the
  same dream-mirrored park, each darker-paletted, with harder Nightmare
  telegraphs and richer fragments. Depths are unlocked by **dream trials**
  (the dream's Mark-of-Mastery idiom: win under a condition — no Surge,
  full-Guard clear, a Spirit-only round).
- At each Sleeping World's deepest floor sleeps its **Sleeping Keyhole**.
  Sealing it **quiets** that World — a dream-seal, felt only in the dream
  (§5's communal line holds: the waking World neither knows nor cares),
  with its own ceremony — and it is real progress on the one save: XP,
  a Journal page, a fragment purse.
- Quieting every current park-mirror completes **"the Realm Rests"** — the
  true ending: a finale ceremony, a permanent Journal frontispiece, and a
  title. Seasonal re-stirring (the real parks' own seasonal turnover, 17
  §4.3, mirrored down) reopens the realm each season without voiding the
  ending — the ending is an achievement, not a subscription.
- **The live feed stays the heartbeat at every depth:** a real breach
  echoes at whatever depth you currently walk — progression changes the
  _stage_ on which the same live spectacle lands, so a veteran dreamer and
  a new one both feel the real park convulse, differently.

### 5b.3 The fulfillment audit

Every dimension a player could seek, served without a turnstile:

| Dimension             | Served in the dream by                                                                                                                                         |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Mastery**           | the same three-verb combat against escalating Nightmare telegraphs; depth trials                                                                               |
| **Progression**       | depths → Sleeping Keyholes → the Realm Rests                                                                                                                   |
| **Collection**        | the Spirit bestiary and the Journal's Nightmare/depth chapters — **100 % completable from home, by rule** (Heartless chapters are the park's; the book is one) |
| **Narrative closure** | the dream arc (§10 delta: the story bible gains it) with a designed ending                                                                                     |
| **Identity**          | the Dive's choice, the Spirit party build, dream cosmetics from fragments                                                                                      |
| **Social belonging**  | dream-vigil aggregates ("1,100 hearts fought for Fantasyland tonight"); received Wayfinders as a _gift_, never a dependency                                    |
| **Spectacle**         | dream-echoes and Nightmare surges cast by real park anomalies, live                                                                                            |

The audit is the acceptance test: if a future feature adds a fulfillment
dimension to the waking game (a new collection track, a new trial type),
the dream game must either gain its own expression of it or record —
explicitly, in this doc's successor — why that dimension is
waking-only.

### 5b.4 What this does _not_ change

Park presence keeps everything §5 gave it: the communal monopoly (seals,
light, weaves, emblems), the full XP rate and condition trophies, Disney
companions, signature keychains, waking-trial regalia, gate-opening and
Wayfinder-_sending_. The never-visitor's ending is whole — and standing in
the real park the morning a parade goes by remains a strictly larger life.
The game's honesty about that gap — advertising it with longing instead of
paywalling it with locks — is the whole design.

## 6. Gates, corridors, and limited teleports (the linkage dig)

KH's travel-tech inventory, mined for the right fit:

| Tech                      | Canon                                                      | Fit                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Corridors of Darkness** | Organization teleportation; corrosive without a black coat | the _name_ players know; right for the scarce/costly framing, wrong alignment for friendly use — reserve for Rift fiction |
| **Lanes Between**         | wielder routes between worlds (BBS)                        | background fiction for the starmap; not a mechanic                                                                        |
| **Keyblade-opened gates** | KH2: the Keyblade unlocks routes between worlds            | ✅ the park-side mechanism: a wielder _opens the way_                                                                     |
| **Sleeping Keyholes**     | DDD: unlock a Sleeping World's depths                      | ✅ dream-side progression gates (not teleports)                                                                           |
| **Wayfinders**            | BBS: friendship charms — "an unbreakable connection"       | ✅ **the teleport token.** Made to link separated friends; this is the entire feature in one licensed object              |
| **Drop**                  | DDD: forced switch between sleeping/waking                 | session fiction, optional                                                                                                 |

### 6.1 Gates are real places

A **gate** is a registered landmark in a real park — the castle, the land
archways, the train-station threshold (curated list; a bounded registry like
Lucky Emblems, [GDD §3.7]). Gates render on both sides: in the waking park
as a subtle shimmer on the living map; in the Sleeping World as the same
spot, _lit_, because someone real is standing there.

### 6.2 What a park wielder does at a gate

1. **Open the way** (free, communal): for ~15 minutes the gate becomes a
   window — dreamers currently diving that park's Sleeping World see the
   _real_ living map through it (Vigil-grade: live aggregates, real
   events, never identities). Teleportation of **sight**, v0 of the link.
2. **Send a Wayfinder** (the limited teleport): a presence-minted token
   (capped: ~1/day in park, small inventory) addressed to a **named
   friend**. The friend, from anywhere, may spend it to **project through
   the gate**: a spectral presence on the real park's living map for a
   bounded window (~10 min) — they see everything live, they can move
   their spectre, emote, and light a **lantern** beside the sender (a
   cosmetic glow both of them see; nobody else does). If the sender enters
   a battle, the friend watches it live, round by round (the `RoundEvent[]`
   stream again — priority ①'s integrity work becomes a _broadcast
   format_).

**No waking verbs through the gate. Period (v1).** A projection cannot
fight, seal, resonate, weave, or affect World light. The teleport is
_social presence_ — being there with someone — not remote play. Grandma
watches her grandkid beat a Berserker outside Space Mountain from her
couch, lights a lantern, and the kid sees it. That's the feature.

### 6.3 Why the scarce thing is park-minted

The user's requirement — "properly rewarding the players actually at the
park" — is solved structurally, not numerically: **the link itself is the
park reward.** Park players don't just earn more; they hold the _keys to
the bridge_. Every Wayfinder received at home is simultaneously a gift, an
ad for presence, and proof that someone at the park thought of you — the
strongest Call (17 §4.4) we could design, and it costs the economy nothing
because it grants no power in either realm.

### 6.4 The 17 §5 refusal, revisited honestly

Refusal #5 ("no remote verbs") **stands for the waking World** — nothing
here touches it. The carve-out is: (a) verbs in a _separate realm_ whose
canonical physics say the waking world doesn't feel them, and (b)
sight/social projection through gates. The real danger is not this design
— it's **drift**: the future pressure to let "just a small" assist verb
through the gate, then a remote seal "only during Convergences," until
presence is a preference. So the canon delta includes a **ratchet rule**:
any future waking-side effect originating from a non-present player
requires its own Canon Decision Log entry with an explicit equity analysis
against pillar 3 — the same ceremony as a balance change, permanently.

## 7. What this does to retention (17, upgraded)

Doc 17's model holds; this fills its weakest cells:

- **The Long Dark** gets a _game_, not just sight: dive nightly if you
  like, raise Spirits, fight the dream-echo when the real park convulses.
  The Vestibule becomes Traverse Town — a place, not a menu.
- **The Dreamer segment** (never visited) stops being a funnel stage and
  becomes a **served player**: Dive → Traverse Town → depths → the Realm
  Rests is a complete arc with no ticket purchase on its critical path
  (§5b). The funnel still exists — a Wayfinder from a friend, the longing
  the dream deliberately cultivates — but conversion is a _hoped-for
  outcome_, never the design's load-bearing assumption. (This resolves 17
  §9's open question about the Dreamer Vestibule: the line between
  anticipation and advertising is that the Dreamer's game is whole either
  way.)
- **The Call** gains its sharpest instruments: a Spirit that can only wake
  at Animal Kingdom is a _reason on the calendar_; a received Wayfinder is
  a person saying "wish you were here" in playable form.
- **Afterglow**: the Chronicle (17 §4.1) gains a dream epilogue — waking
  bonds formed this trip (§9 D4), gates opened, who projected in.

## 8. The brutal list (risks and honest costs)

- **License scope (gate zero, restated).** Nationwide KH is a different
  deal than in-park KH. No design below matters until this clears. Output
  of this doc usable in that conversation: the Dreamscape is _promotional
  by construction_ (every surface points at park visitation) — that is the
  pitch to both licensors.
- **Art is the real cost — and §5b raised the floor.** A Nightmare/Spirit
  bestiary in the DDD idiom is a second art program on top of 16 §9's
  pipeline, and a _complete_ dream game (depths, keyhole ceremonies, the
  Realm Rests finale, a closable bestiary) cannot ship as sparse as a
  companion surface could have. Phasing (§9) meters it: D1 still ships
  with dream-palette variants of existing Heartless models (canonically
  defensible: Nightmares mimic) and the Spirit line starts at 3–5 species
  — but the D2/D2.5 bill is now a known, committed cost, not an optional
  extension. Completeness is bought in content, and this line is where
  it's paid.
- **Infra scale.** Away DAU decouples from park attendance — SSE fanout,
  battle-replay writes, and push volume get a national ceiling instead of
  a turnstile ceiling. The dream realm needs its own channel namespace
  (dream events never ride the park channel — vocabulary is park-scoped
  canon) and a cost model _before_ D1 ships, not after.
- **Cannibalization** — "if the dream is good, why fly?" Sharpened by the
  one-game model: the dream now advances the same save, so the answer
  rests entirely on §5's four asymmetries (communal monopoly, rate +
  exclusives, the keys, the longing) and on tuning the dream rate low
  enough that a park day stays the biggest day of the year. Empirically
  checkable: 17 §6's re-trip attach rate is the guard metric, now watched
  per-knob (if raising the dream XP fraction moves re-trip down, the knob
  turns back). If dream-heavy users re-trip _less_, the design failed and
  D-phases stop.
- **COPPA / families**: 16 §7.2's compliance gap is now national-scale and
  pre-park (Dreamer children). It moves from "design gap" to "launch
  blocker" for D1.
- **Safety posture actually improves**: the Dreamscape is a couch
  experience by design — no sidewalk gameplay, no traffic, no
  trespass-to-spawn. The waking game's in-park safety canon (09) is
  untouched.
- **Moderation surface: unchanged.** No new UGC anywhere in this doc —
  Spirits are forged from catalogs, gates are curated, projection has no
  free input (emotes + the lantern). This is deliberate and load-bearing.

## 9. Phasing (if adopted — each independently shippable)

- **D0 — the Dive ships** (16 §1, already proposed): the realm's front
  door exists regardless of this doc's fate.
- **D1 — the Dreamscape MVP.** Dream-mirror of the user's most-visited
  park on the ②b stage (dream shader); dream-echoes of real live breaches;
  Nightmare palette-variants; **real XP at the dream rate into the one
  save**. Zero new battle code, zero geodata — but a **hard dependency on
  priority ① (sessions + server replay + caps)**, because desk-playable
  fights that mint real XP cannot ship client-trusted even for a day
  (§5's integrity consequence). _Otherwise assembled from parts the
  adopted order already builds._
- **D2 — Spirits.** Forge/raise loop, one-roster integration, the
  Journal's Nightmare chapters. (First new system; the art program's
  first real bill.)
- **D2.5 — the whole game.** Depths + dream trials + Sleeping Keyholes +
  the Realm Rests ceremony (§5b). This is the completeness milestone: from
  here, a never-visitor has a full arc. Ships before Gates on purpose —
  **the never-visitor's game is complete before the bridge to the park
  gets its marquee feature.**
- **D3 — Gates.** Open-the-way windows, then Wayfinder projection.
  Depends: SSE wire ③, presence M5b, FCM A4 — all already on the board.
- **D4 — Waking bonds.** A Spirit brought (in its wielder's pocket) to
  the real World it dreams of forms a **waking bond** — a permanent
  passive amplifier, the dream's version of the companions' home-World
  passive. A park _moment_, not an unlock: Spirits fight everywhere from

## 11. Open questions

- **The license conversation** (gate zero): what scope does "companion
  experience" cover in the existing agreement, and is the
  promotional-by-construction framing enough for both licensors?
- **Spirit depth**: raising mechanics risk sliding toward the
  pet-collection games pillar 6 exists to avoid — where exactly is the
  line between "forge and train" and "creature game"?
- **Can two people share one dream?** (Co-op dives — deferred; pillar 2
  says solo-complete first, and shared dreams multiply moderation and
  infra questions.)
- **Dream difficulty**: largely answered by §5b (depths + dream trials +
  Sleeping Keyholes); what remains is tuning — how steep the depth curve
  runs, and whether trial conditions rotate.
- **The completeness/cannibalization tension, sharpened**: §5b makes the
  home game whole, which makes 17 §6's re-trip guard metric _more_
  load-bearing, not less. If the data ever shows the whole-game cohort
  converting to trips at a lower rate than the pre-§5b design would have,
  which promise wins? (Position taken here: completeness wins — §5b.4's
  longing-not-locks stance is the brand-safe answer — but it should be
  decided consciously at adoption, not discovered in a dashboard.)
- **Does the pedometer trickle (steps → dream links) need its own equity
  review?** Walking is available to almost everyone, but not everyone —
  accessibility posture needed before it ships.
